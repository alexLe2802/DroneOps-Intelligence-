II. Software Requirement Specification
1. Product Overview
DroneOps Intelligence manages UAVs, pilot accounts and flight credentials, mission planning and reviewed updates, configured flight restrictions, live telemetry, incidents, post-flight evidence and historical-mission-based decision support. The Web application supports Operators and Operations Managers. The Operator mobile client covers account/profile access, own/assigned mission viewing, validation and decision results, live monitoring, alerts and history; mission route editing and Manager administration are Web functions.
The platform validates routes against configured restrictions before Manager approval. A MAVLink-compatible UAV/autopilot or simulator supplies telemetry through an integration gateway. The platform detects configured abnormal conditions, retains evidence and generates post-flight reports. AI decision support retrieves similar historical flights and their incident evidence; a configured OpenAI or Gemini service may summarize that evidence. AI advice never replaces mandatory validation or Manager decisions.
Scope exclusions: no low-level navigation, stabilization, autonomous flight execution or replacement of GCS/autopilot software. Custom model training and autonomous AI approval are outside scope. Restriction checks cover configured data only; they do not certify compliance with unprovided restrictions.

3.1.3 Screen Authorization
Authorization policy: Operators access missions they own or are assigned to; Managers access records within their managed operational scope. Both UI and backend enforce these rules. Administrative registration/license review is assigned to the Operations Manager role; an implementation role named admin receives these actions only through explicit authorization. Read access does not grant permission to modify evidence.

3.1.7 Lifecycle Diagrams
A new mission begins in Draft. Initial submission triggers validation and Manager review. Every subsequent accepted pre-flight edit creates a pending update for the same mission and automatically triggers validation and review. The current mission remains unchanged while review is pending. The lifecycle and concurrency rules are defined in BR-13 and CR-05.

Figure 13. Mission lifecycle.

Figure 14. Pending update request lifecycle.
Approval atomically applies proposed content to the same mission ID. Rejecting an update preserves current content and operational status, including an existing Approved status. A mission with a pending update cannot begin execution. Cancellation affects the planning record and never sends a flight-control command.

FR-23.1 — Normal flow

5. Requirement Appendix
5.1 Business Rules
ID
Rule Definition
BR-01
Every human request is authenticated and authorized for the action and record scope; external telemetry/AI integrations use configured integration credentials, not human login.
BR-02
UAVs and pilots must be active and eligible. A pilot license must be approved, unrevoked and valid through the scheduled flight end. Deactivation preserves history and is blocked for an active flight. Assignment conflicts follow BR-14.
BR-03
Approval applies only to the reviewed mission or proposed update after mandatory spatial checks pass with current evidence. Applying a proposal updates the same mission atomically. Incomplete/error/stale validation cannot authorize approval.
BR-04
Both waypoints and connecting route segments shall be validated against active restrictions using their configured coordinate units, altitude reference and boundary rules.
BR-05
Initial mission submission and every later pre-flight edit require Manager review. Initial Draft creation is not submission. No application action issues low-level flight-control commands. Lifecycle rules follow BR-13.
BR-06
Mission changes, validation runs and decisions link to mission ID and update request ID. Every accepted edit requires Manager review. Approved current content and status remain unchanged until a proposal is approved; rejected proposals never overwrite them. Earlier evidence cannot authorize changed content.
BR-07
Telemetry and displayed summaries retain source/received timestamps and missing-data indicators. Unknown or stale measurements must not appear as fresh valid data.
BR-08
Communication loss shall be evaluated by an independent timer as well as on packet arrival. Timeout and recovery conditions shall be configurable.
BR-09
Incidents shall retain type, time, UAV/mission association and frozen event evidence. Acknowledgement and resolution shall record actor, timestamp and reason without rewriting source telemetry.
BR-10
Post-flight reports and analytics shall use stored mission, telemetry and incident evidence and show missing data and coverage explicitly. Calculated metrics shall retain their units and covered time window.
BR-11
AI output provides mission summaries, risk observations and precautions for human review. It does not approve/reject a mission or command a UAV. API failure must not block manual review.
BR-12
Record actor/integration identity, action, target and timestamp for material changes; protect credentials and restrict source context sent to AI to permitted mission data.
BR-13
Mission lifecycle: Draft -> Pending Review -> Approved or Rejected. Draft/Rejected may be initially submitted; each accepted edit to a non-executing mission creates a pending update without changing current content/status. Update approval applies content and sets Approved; update rejection preserves current content/status. A newer accepted proposal supersedes the previous pending review. Draft/Pending Review/Rejected/Approved may be cancelled; executing or terminal missions cannot be edited. Flight start requires Approved, fresh validation, eligible assignments and no pending update. A trusted start event creates one flight and moves the mission to In Progress; a trusted completion event moves it to Completed. Reflight requires a new mission.
BR-14
Schedules use UTC instants and display the user time zone. End must exceed start. Approved missions reserve UAV/pilot intervals [start,end); adjacent intervals do not conflict. Pending submissions/proposals are checked again atomically at approval and flight start. A proposal cannot reserve over another approved booking; its existing approved reservation is replaced only on approval.
BR-15
Default detector settings: communication loss after 10 seconds without a valid mapped sample, checked at least once per second; recovery requires two consecutive valid samples. Battery below 20% for two consecutive samples is Warning; recovery requires two samples at or above 25%. Altitude outside the approved/configured MSL range and speed above the configured UAV maximum for two consecutive samples are Critical; recovery requires two samples within limits. Communication loss is Critical. Missing thresholds or altitude conversion disable the affected rule with visible diagnostics. Manager-configured changes are audited; severity escalations update the same incident with retained evidence.
BR-16
The author cannot approve their own proposed mission update or license-change request. Another authorized Manager must review it. No mission may start with a pending proposal; no flight-control action is issued to enforce this planning decision.
BR-17
Keep current account email and approved license until replacement verification/review succeeds. A rejected or cancelled license request does not modify current credentials. Expiry of the current license still affects eligibility while a replacement is pending.
BR-18
Historical similarity retrieval uses up to five completed flights in the authorized data scope. Normalize numeric features with a recorded feature schema and score method; display fewer than five when fewer eligible records exist. Store feature/index configuration and evidence IDs with each assessment. AI provider output cannot change approval or detector thresholds.

5.5 Terms and Data Conventions
Mission: the current planning record with one stable ID. Mission update request: a separately stored proposed change requiring review, not an executable mission or mission version. Flight: the immutable execution record created from an approved mission at start.
MSL: altitude above mean sea level. AGL: altitude above local ground; conversion requires terrain/reference data. WGS84: geographic coordinate reference for latitude and longitude.
Fresh telemetry: valid mapped data received within the configured communication-loss threshold. Stale/offline: the threshold has elapsed; last-known values remain visible with timestamps. An incident episode persists until measured recovery.
Top-k retrieval: selecting up to k relevant completed flights using a recorded feature representation and similarity method. Similarity is not a calibrated probability of safety or incident occurrence.
